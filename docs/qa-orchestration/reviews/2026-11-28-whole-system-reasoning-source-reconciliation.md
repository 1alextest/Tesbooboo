## Precedent and principle distillation (candidate additions, not installed)

- [OSLC specifications](https://www.open-services.net/specifications/) and [OASIS configuration management](https://www.oasis-open.org/standards/) distinguish independently owned engineering resources, cross-domain links and versioned configurations. Transfer: compose a coherent reference basis across domain owners; do not centralize their mutation authority. Failure mode: resolving a floating current revision in a historical assessment, broken/inaccessible external links, incompatible configurations. Recovery: re-resolve scoped version references, mark incomplete or stale, retain historical basis. Alternative: existing Tesbo relational references and adapters, without adopting RDF/OSLC protocol. No evidence that a technology migration is necessary.
- [W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/) separates entities, activities, agents, generation, derivation and invalidation. Transfer: preserve what was used, who/what produced it, how it was derived and when reliance ceases to be valid. Failure: a model-generated summary treated as an authoritative original or a human issuer treated as executor. Recovery: inspect original revision/actor/receipt, qualify or invalidate dependent claims. Reuse existing native audit/attempt/version owners. Neither standard establishes an AI model's semantic correctness or Tesbo's benefit; no technology adoption decided.

**Candidate principle A — evidence-bound cross-domain reasoning:** derive the relevant scope and authority from the question, subject and quality obligation, not the requesting interface or fixed artifact inventory. Compose authorized versioned facts, observations and relationships from existing owners; preserve differences between normative and observed truth, provenance and missing scope. Falsifier: a policy/manual-only change and a code-only change both resolve the applicable evidence, without forcing unrelated records or treating denied access as no obligation. Proposed home: canonical 00 (product doctrine), refine R-ASE-02 and FR-115–124 in 01, realization in 03/04, acceptance in 05; 06 references method rather than forks binding rule.

**Candidate principle B — separate semantic identity from representation:** preserve the distinction between a subject, authoritative versioned artifact reference, typed relationship, activity/event/decision, and the assertion made about them. These are conceptual roles, not five new database tables. Actors, authority, scope, time, provenance and evidence are constraints that may themselves reference existing entities. Do not flatten divergent lifecycles into a universal artifact CRUD/status. Falsifier: a requirement revision, code commit, actual execution, user feedback and approval decision retain independent authority and lifecycle while connected to the same change. Proposed home: 03 existing graph/Change Packet; 01 R-ASE-01/05.

**Candidate principle C — qualified negative space:** absence is only a defensible finding relative to an evidenced obligation, explicit bounded search universe and sufficient visibility/freshness. Otherwise return an unresolved candidate or unknown. Falsifier: inaccessible, stale, partial and complete-empty inventory yield different authorized outcomes; no leak of restricted existence. Proposed home: 06 evidence-grounded method, 01/05 existing FR-124 and R-ASE-02 evaluation/acceptance.

**Candidate principle D — invalidate reliance, not history:** when material evidence, approved basis, scope, effective configuration or entitlement changes, recompute affected reliance through bounded dependency traversal; preserve the original receipt but enforce current authorization even for historical access. Falsifier: prior conclusion remains historical, current action refuses stale basis, unrelated receipt remains valid, and revocation prevents disclosure even from cache/export. Proposed home: 03/04 existing graph and publication contracts, 05 qualification.

## Artifact roles and extensibility

A type inventory is a discovery aid, not the architecture. Stakeholder outcomes and quality concerns (accessibility, usability, security, privacy, performance, reliability, resilience, interoperability, maintainability, recoverability, observability, data integrity, compliance, testability) can be subjects/obligations/attributes crossing requirements, policies, design, implementation, observations, test definitions and results. They are not automatically modules. A design specification, API/data/interface contract, configuration, infrastructure or dependency version may be a governed document, native resource, source-bound reference or observed representation; a design change is an event/change reference, not itself a design artifact. Incidents/feedback/logs/metrics/traces may be observations or external references with retention/privacy constraints, not normative truth. Risks, exceptions, decisions, release records and work items retain existing authority, lifecycle and evidentiary role. Explicitly discover applicable unlisted concepts rather than silently dropping them. No new universal artifact registry proposed.
