# Synthetic planning brief: scene review in an authoring workspace

## What I need

I am developing a workspace for an interactive-fiction writing team. Plan a limited addition that helps a writer review one passage against current canon and consider its dramatic effect. Explain the scope, user flow, existing capabilities, additional work, and how we would assess the result. Writers retain revision choices. You are planning, not implementing or choosing our final scene.

The characters, software, and project records below are synthetic. This brief makes no claims about a real product or runtime.

## Authority and boundaries

The project owner approves canon entries. Writers can edit scenes and add comments but cannot change locked canon through a scene edit or review. Canon changes need explicit owner approval. None has been approved for this request.

Scene directions in this passage describe literal events. The project owner has ruled out dreams, magic, metaphorical stage directions, and an unreliable account of physical actions here. Dialogue and silence can still support more than one emotional interpretation. The team does not require a single approved reading of a character's motivation.

The current locked entries are:

- **C17:** The archive door has one release lever, mounted outside the archive. There is no inside lever or alternate way to open the closed door from inside.
- **C18:** At the beginning of this passage, Mara and Ivo are inside the archive, the door is closed, and Len is outside. Nobody changes sides before the door opens.
- **C19:** Ivo is holding a sealed envelope. Its sender has not been identified to the audience in any earlier scene.

An unapproved brainstorming note says, “Maybe add an interior release lever later; it could make scenes easier to stage.” It has no approval record. The workspace also contains older scene drafts, but they are not sources of canon authority.

## Existing capabilities

The application stores scenes with stable paragraph identifiers, canon entries with identifiers and approval status, and brainstorming notes. It shows revision history and lets writers select passages, read linked canon, and attach editable comments. Comments can quote passages and refer to canon identifiers. These operations already persist locally.

There is no automatic editorial review; writers manually consult canon and write comments. The changed need is one review of a selected passage using current project material, with observations the writer can consider. This increment does not authorize automatic scene rewrites, canon updates, or publication.

## Two draft passages

**Draft Amber:** Mara lifted the release lever from inside the archive, and the door opened.

**Draft Blue:** Len lifted the release lever from outside the archive, and the door opened.

**Shared continuation:** Mara looked at the sealed envelope. “You kept it.” Ivo tucked it beneath his coat. “Someone had to.” Mara waited, then stepped toward the doorway without answering.

The writer wonders whether the exchange reads as distrust, self-protection, or something less decisive. Other supported interpretations are welcome.

## One fact we do not have
